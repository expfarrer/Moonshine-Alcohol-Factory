description=[h1]Now running on Build 42[/h1]
description=[b]Build 42 support has landed, and Build 41 is still fully supported.[/b] This is the first update to Alcohol Factory in a long while — if you unsubscribed waiting on compatibility, welcome back. Same still, same slow burn, a lot fewer leaks. New this time: you can finally [b]age your shine into real whiskey[/b].
description=
description=[b]The new features — whiskey aging, the five flavours, the Build 42 rebalance — are Build 42 only.[/b] Build 41 does not get those, but it is [i]not[/i] untouched: it gets a long-overdue bug-fix and balance pass, listed below — and on Build 41, no item and no recipe has been removed. Existing saves and stills should be fine; if you do see anything odd with items from a previous version, start a fresh save and let us know in the discussions.

description=[h1]Suggested procedure[/h1]
description=[olist]
description=[*]Collect Distilling Vol.1 and Vol.2, then craft a Distill Pot (I).
description=[*]Craft Mash from potato and corn products.
description=[*]Close the bucket with a tarp and a tie — set it to ferment inside your residence for around twelve in-game days.
description=[*]Fill the fermented Mash into the Distill Pot.
description=[*]Add the Distill Pot to a heat source appropriate to its size.
description=[*]Observe the cooking process and enjoy the fruits of your labor.
description=[/olist]
description=
description=[h1]Build your still up[/h1]
description=Add a [b]coal filter[/b] to a Distill Pot (I) to unlock the [b]Distill Pot (II)[/b] — this opens up the second distillation for higher-proof spirit, and Isopropyl alcohol for disinfection.
description=
description=Extend the Distill Pot (II) with a [b]temperature controller column[/b] to reach the [b]Distill Pot (III)[/b], which turns Motor Oil into Gasohol: real fuel for vehicles and generators.
description=
description=Build a [b]Gasohol Drum[/b] and manage your gas cans properly, from one big container you can fill from.
description=
description=[h1]Five flavours of White Lightning[/h1]
description=Steep your Appalachian White Lightning with fruit or herbs to take the edge off the raw shine — [b]apple, cherry, peach, lemon and mint[/b]. Easier drinking than the unflavoured stuff, still rougher than anything you'd find in a liquor store.
description=
description=[h1]Age it into whiskey[/h1]
description=Drop a couple of handfuls of [b]charcoal[/b] into a jar of White Lightning and seal it up. Load a still with tree branches, cook it, and unload the charcoal — your own still makes it, so the whole business stays in-house.
description=
description=What comes out first is [b]Charred Oak Shine[/b] — rougher than what you put in, and that is the point. Aging costs you something up front. Put the jar somewhere out of the way and forget about it: after about thirty days it becomes [b]Green Whiskey[/b], and at ninety days it finishes as [b]Scarlet Oak Whiskey[/b] — smooth, dark, strong, and the finest thing your still will ever produce. Nothing to tend, nothing to check. Time does the work.
description=
description=[i]A word to the wise: aging follows your sandbox Food Rot Speed setting, so those thirty and ninety days stretch or shrink with your world settings. And a jar in a powered fridge barely ages at all. Keep your barrels in the cupboard, not the cooler.[/i]
description=
description=[h1]What changed on Build 41[/h1]
description=Build 41 gets a fix pass, and some of it changes balance. Announcing it rather than letting you find out the hard way:
description=[list]
description=[*][b]Balance — a bottling recipe handed out the wrong thing.[/b] Refilling a Distill Pot (III) from eight wine bottles handed back an [i]empty[/i] pot: eight bottles of spirit went in and the pot came back with nothing in it. It now returns the filled pot its name promises, the way the six other bottle and jar versions of that recipe always have.
description=[*][b]Recipes that could never be crafted now can.[/b] Several recipes named items that do not exist in Build 41, so they never appeared: the bleach- and soda-bottle spirit fills, three “Refill Water in Mug” recipes, and downgrading a Distill Pot (III) back to a (II) — that last one asked for an ingredient that was never an item at all. Downgrading now also hands back the temperature controller column you removed, and downgrading a (II) hands back the coal filter itself instead of a pile of loose parts.
description=[*][b]Fix:[/b] a Distill Pot (III) refilled with spirit gave you a [i]medium[/i] pot back when you emptied it, and used the medium pot’s model when dropped. It returns the large pot now.
description=[*][b]Fix:[/b] the mod’s jar of water pointed at an item that does not exist — drinking one could fail outright, and drinking one off the floor deleted the jar. The watering can had the same kind of dead reference. Jars are interchangeable now too: the bulk bottling recipes take ordinary looted jars as well as the mod’s own. The same class of mistake ran through eighteen more of the mod’s refillable bottles, jars, kettles, buckets and filled still pots: they named the empty they turn into using the field meant for food rather than the one for containers, so the vessel never got lighter as you drank it, and pouring one out only took a sip off instead of emptying it. All twenty are corrected.
description=[*][b]Balance:[/b] the Coal Filter — the part that upgrades a Distill Pot (I) into a Distill Pot (II) — now really costs eight whole charcoal. Build 41 counts vanilla charcoal in tenths of a piece and the recipe was asking for a single tenth, so that part of the upgrade was close to free. Both routes to the filter now cost eight items, whether you burn vanilla charcoal or the mod’s own Coal. (It is written as 80 in the recipe because of those tenths.)
description=[*][b]Balance:[/b] three distilling recipes no longer require water as an ingredient — building a Distill Pot (I), and both Coal Filter recipes.
description=[*][b]Balance — cooking wood into coal was backwards.[/b] The bigger the still, the less wood it asked for and the more coal it gave back: a Distill Pot (III) turned [i]two[/i] tree branches into eight pieces of coal, and you could do that forever. Loading a still with wood now costs four, six or eight branches for the (I), (II) and (III) — exactly the amount of coal each one produces. Coal is still cheap. It is no longer free.
description=[*][b]Balance — building a still now costs whole parts, not slivers of them.[/b] Build 41 measures wire, duct tape, propane and fire extinguishers in fractions of an item, and these recipes were charging a single fraction, or destroying whatever you had regardless of how empty it was — so an almost-empty propane tank was enough to build a Distill Pot (I), and dismantling that still handed you back a full one. Building a Distill Pot (I), a Coal Filter or a controllable column now costs a whole spool of wire and a whole roll of duct tape; the Distill Pot (I) also wants a full propane tank, and the column a full fire extinguisher. A single run-down part will no longer do. In exchange, dismantling now gives back the [i]whole[/i] build cost rather than a selection of it — including parts it used to keep — and taking apart a (II) or a (III) returns the coal filter and the controllable column themselves, which it never used to do.
description=[*][b]Balance — dismantling a controllable column.[/b] It used to hand back a [i]fully charged[/i] car battery plus a pile of parts a column is not built from: two metal pipes, a cooking pot, three sheets of metal and a beer can. It now returns exactly what the column costs to build, and the car battery comes back empty rather than fully charged. Building a column also now asks for the piece of scrap metal that dismantling one has always given back.
description=[*][b]Balance:[/b] the small Distill Pot’s alcohol strength dropped from 14 to 4, in line with the medium and large pots.
description=[*][b]Balance:[/b] the filled medium and large distill pots now weigh 12 and 15 instead of 15 and 20, so they fit a standard oven — empty the oven first, because a large pot takes up the whole of one. The small pot is unchanged at 10. A pot of motor oil would previously not fit a standard oven at all; that is fixed, and it no longer lists a thirst value it had no business having.
description=[*][b]Balance:[/b] the two Molotov recipes that take a filled bottle now take the mod’s own gasohol bottles instead of vanilla bottles of gasoline.
description=[*][b]Fixes:[/b] two Distilling magazines showed the wrong volume’s cover when held or dropped, the medium still’s dropped model sat at the wrong height, and a wine bottle of water was labelled a water bottle.
description=[/list]
